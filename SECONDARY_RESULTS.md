# Secondary analyses on dev

Generated by `python secondary_analysis.py`. Full tables are in `outputs/secondary_dev.md`.

## Within-question ordering (intended level)

Each question has an Elementary, a High School and a Graduate version. Without fitting any cutoffs, we check whether a measure scores them in that order. Pairs are counted as correct, half (tie) or wrong, so chance is 0.5. "Fully ordered" means all three versions are in the right order (chance 0.167).

| Measure | Adjacent pairs | Distant pair (E–G) | Fully ordered |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| word_count (M0) | 0.812 | 1.000 | 0.625 |
| dale_chall (M1) | 0.800 | 0.988 | 0.600 |
| rare_word_share (M2) | 0.794 | 0.938 | 0.575 |
| flesch_kincaid (M1) | 0.738 | 0.825 | 0.525 |
| rare_term_density (M3) | 0.719 | 0.850 | 0.463 |
| mean_sentence_length (M2) | 0.522 | 0.475 | 0.200 |

- **The generators change the audience mainly through length and vocabulary.** On all 80 dev questions, the Graduate version is longer than the Elementary one. Word-frequency measures also separate the levels well.
- **Sentence length does not change with audience.** Mean sentence length orders the versions at chance (0.52 and 0.48). This goes against the part of the hypothesis that says LLMs adjust sentence length. Formulas that rely heavily on sentence length (Flesch–Kincaid, SMOG, ARI) order the versions worse than word-list measures such as Dale–Chall.
- **Adjacent levels are harder than distant ones for every measure.** This matches the proposal's expectation.

## Pairs with different perceived levels

Among pairs of versions of the same question with different human-perceived levels, there are 118 adjacent pairs and 21 distant pairs. Word count orders 81% of the adjacent pairs correctly. The best non-length measures, Dale–Chall and Reading Ease, reach 75%. Most measures get 95–100% of the distant pairs right, but with only 21 such pairs, these numbers are noisy.

## Bootstrap confidence intervals (perceived level)

We resampled the 80 dev questions 1,000 times, keeping each question's three versions together and holding cutoffs fixed.

- The accuracy intervals are about ±6 points wide (word count: 57.9%, CI [51.2%, 64.2%]). Except for Coleman–Liau, mean word length, mean sentence length, noun-chunk length and the definition ratio, no measure's accuracy is distinguishable from word count. **Accuracy differences on dev should not be over-interpreted.**
- For Spearman ρ, only the M3 measures (`rare_term_density`, `undefined_term_density`) and Dale–Chall have paired intervals against word count that include 0. Every other measure correlates significantly less with the perceived level than length does.
